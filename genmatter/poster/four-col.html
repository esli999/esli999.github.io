Abstract

GenMatter frames perception as Bayesian inference in a generative model of matter: a scene is particles (small Gaussians of local matter) grouped into clusters (independently moveable objects), recovered without learning, through probabilistic inference. One model spans random dots, camouflaged textures, and natural video, where biological vision succeeds but computer vision does not.

One visual system, three worlds

Three worlds, one model: random dots, camouflage, and natural video. Humans see the object in each. No single vision system does.

One model, run both ways: forward, it renders a scene (solid arrows), and inverted, it perceives the objects (hollow arrows).

  • Motion comes in rigid pieces: each object shares one rigid motion, and grouping segments that motion into objects.
  • Domain-general by construction: the same model perceives all three worlds, with no per-task training or hand-crafted regularizers.

The generative matter model

Depth and flow lift every pixel to a moving 3D point, and perception inverts that generative process.

  • Two-level hierarchy with slack: an object is a cluster of particles that can deviate from the cluster’s rigid motion, so one model fits both rigid and deformable objects.
  • Perception is inference: GenMatter infers the hidden objects and their motions, grouping the particles that move as one.

Inference

A training-free inference engine: feature extraction, then block-Gibbs sampling, then Bayesian filtering.

  • Structure makes inference tractable: conditional independence across the hierarchy lets GenMatter sample every level in parallel, so posterior inference runs online, a sweep or two per frame.
  • Deep features for structured inference: pretrained depth, flow, and DINO networks extract the features, and GenMatter’s inference groups the points into objects.
  • Training-free core: with no task-specific finetuning, a vectorized GenJAX engine runs on one L4 GPU in a few kilobytes of code.

Human object perception

Stimulus

GenMatter inference

150 participants judged whether two probe dots share an object across 27 kinematograms. GenMatter matches them at r2 = 0.86.

  • Cluster grouping is essential: without the cluster layer, 5- and 500-particle variants reach only r2 = 0.35 / 0.41, with learned FlowSAM at r2 = 0.04, far below the full model.
  • Captures graded uncertainty: the model’s confidence tracks how often people group the dots, staying calibrated even on the hardest, most ambiguous trials.

Structure from motion in camouflage

Find the moving object: each panel is a single texture frame, where only motion reveals the hidden 3D object.

Motion reveals 3D shape. GenMatter recovers the hidden shape and mask where learned baselines drift or fail.

  • Structure from motion, not appearance: GenMatter recasts the Gestalt principle of common fate as Bayesian inference, finding objects that no appearance-based feedforward model can see.
  • Outperforms the learned baselines: higher Jaccard than FlowSAM on 111 of 140 videos and SegAnyMo on 133 of 140, even with depth ablated and motion as the only cue.

Conclusion

  • One model, many viewing conditions: a single probabilistic engine spans random dots, camouflage, and natural video, approaching the generality of biological vision that no single learning system has matched.
  • Bridges human vision and computer vision: object-level scene understanding emerges from structured inference.
